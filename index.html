Why it keeps coming back

Most approaches treat pain as an event to manage instead of a pattern to fix. That is usually why relief never lasts.

Rest

Pain fades for a few days off your feet, then comes right back the moment you return to normal life, because nothing about how you move actually changed.

Generic fitness programs

Built for a body that does not have your specific compensation patterns. Some exercises can quietly reinforce the exact imbalance causing the pain.

Physical therapy alone

Effective for managing an acute injury. But most plans end the day you are discharged, with no strategy for staying that way once you are back to daily life.

Questions people ask before booking

Is this physical therapy?+
No. REP Athletics provides movement coaching and training, not physical therapy or medical treatment. It is designed to complement medical care, not replace it.
What if exercise usually makes my pain worse?+
That is one of the most common reasons people come to us. Every plan starts with a real assessment of how you move, so your program is built around your body instead of a generic routine that could aggravate things further.
Do I need to already be in good shape to start?+
No. Clients range from weekend warriors and desk workers to adults returning to movement after a setback. Programs are built around your current capacity, not a fitness level you're expected to already have.
How soon will I notice a difference?+
It depends on your starting point and consistency. Many clients notice changes in how they move within the first few weeks, with more significant progress typically over three to six months. Individual results vary.
I've already tried physical therapy or chiropractic care. Is this different?+
Yes. We regularly work with clients transitioning out of physical therapy who want a real plan for what comes after "you're discharged," rather than being left to figure it out alone.
What actually happens at the first assessment?+
A trainer looks at how you move, where compensation patterns are showing up, and what is likely contributing to your pain, then outlines a plan built around that, not a template.
